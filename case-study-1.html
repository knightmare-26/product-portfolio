Case study CS-01

Spotify "Nearby" — context-aware local music discovery

Spotify knows your taste. It doesn't know you just landed in Barcelona. This is a self-initiated exploration of what closing that gap looks like — as one new surface, not a redesign.

The gap I noticed

When I travel somewhere new, Spotify keeps recommending music based on my existing taste. It has no way of telling me what's actually being listened to there, or what local scene I'm walking into. Spotify already uses location for event discovery and some localized catalog surfacing — but local context has never been a first-class input to core music discovery, sitting alongside personal taste.

Framing question: How might Spotify combine a user's personal music taste with their local context to improve discovery — without compromising personalization or privacy?

Why a global taste model can't fake this

Point the idea at four cities and you get four unrelated musical identities, not just different trending songs:

  • Barcelona — Catalan indie, flamenco-fusion, electronic sets tied to the club scene
  • Nashville — country, Americana, the songwriter-circuit and bluegrass crossovers
  • Tokyo — city-pop revival, J-rock, idol comebacks, Japanese hip-hop
  • Lagos — Afrobeats and amapiano crossovers, singles that break locally before they chart globally

Different genre identity, instrumentation, and cultural function of music. A recommendation model tuned on aggregate global taste has no way to distinguish these contexts unless local signal is built in as its own input. The product becomes personal taste × local context, not taste alone.

Who it's for

  • Travelers — visiting a new city; want to feel the local music culture without knowing what to search for.
  • Local explorers — long-time residents whose recommendations have gone stale; want what's emerging around them.
  • Event / nightlife users — deciding what to do tonight; want the music before the event listing.

These are reasoned from the core idea, not from interviews — validating them with real travelers and locals is the first real step before building.

The MVP: "Nearby"

Scoped deliberately to a single new discovery surface, not a platform change. It has four parts:

  • Trending near you — what the city is actually streaming right now.
  • Local artists — acts rooted in the place, including "big here, unknown elsewhere" ones.
  • Your local mix — a personalized playlist blending your taste with local trends.
  • Playing tonight — nearby shows, with a track playing first so the decision isn't blind.

I prototyped it inside Spotify's real interface language (shelves, mix card, mini-player, tab bar) so it reads as a native extension, not a separate app. The prototype runs the actual recommendation logic described below. Switch the city and the screen changes, and so does every track's score.

How the local signal works

For every city with enough active users, Spotify computes a rolling local listening index — the most-streamed tracks and artists by users whose device location resolves to that city over a trailing 7-day window. This is a population-level signal, not a personalized one: it needs to know where streams come from in aggregate, never who any individual listener is. That index is then blended with the visiting or resident user's own taste profile.

The population-level design is deliberate — it's what makes the privacy model workable, and it's what makes cold start and popularity bias real constraints to design around rather than edge cases.

The recommendation blend — a hypothesis, not a result

40%personal listening history
25%local popularity
20%taste similarity
15%freshness + context

These numbers are a guess about the shape of the trade-off, not a benchmarked model: taste should still dominate, and local context should meaningfully move the needle without overwhelming personalization. The real weighting would only emerge from experimentation.

In the live prototype you can move these weights yourself, along with every rule below (cold start, artist cap, long-tail reserve, rotation, privacy tiers), and see a per-track breakdown of the scores.

Privacy, as a design constraint

Location control is four tiers — Never / Approximate / While using Spotify / Always — and the default is approximate ("Barcelona", not coordinates). Because the local index is aggregate, no individual's listening history is ever exposed to surface local recommendations, and there's a one-tap opt-out on the surface itself.

How I'd measure it

North star — incremental music discovery: the share of users who meaningfully engage with a new artist or song they found through Nearby. Not raw click volume — discovery of music people actually keep listening to.

Control gets normal recommendations; treatment gets normal recommendations plus Nearby. Guardrail metrics: skip rate, hide / not-interested, location opt-out, recommendation dissatisfaction.

Risks I designed around

  • Cold start in small cities → fall back to regional aggregation, and say so in the UI ("trending in Catalonia") rather than faking city-level signal.
  • Popularity bias → cap the slots one dominant artist can take; reserve ~30% for long-tail local acts.
  • Tourist mismatch → only weight local signal heavily after 24–48h in-market, or let users self-declare "traveling" vs "local".
  • Echo chamber → rotate the local mix weekly, and inject novelty through taste-similarity rather than pure popularity.

What I'd validate next

  • The three user segments — with real travelers and residents, before any build.
  • Whether the local layer earns engagement incremental to Discover Weekly, or just cannibalizes it.
  • The privacy framing — does "approximate location" actually reassure people, or does any location ask suppress opt-in?
